## How to read this

- **supported**: a document in `docs/nasa/` backs the sentence (citation given). For a "what" sentence, it means the sentence states only what the rule computes.
- **rewrite**: not backed by our sources, or slightly inaccurate. A general, safe replacement is proposed. "Keep" means the text is already general and safe, and there is nothing to cite.
- **unsafe**: an operational procedure, a medication instruction, a diagnosis-like claim, a claim our own source contradicts, or a promise the system does not keep.

### Cross-cutting: "The flight surgeon has been notified."

This sentence ends 21 alert "do" messages: 12 hand-written red rules, plus the 9 nutrition alerts through the generated `NOTIFIED` suffix. `radiation-career-alert` and `behavioral-health-alert` use variants. It is a statement about the system, not a NASA claim. It is backed by SPEC §6.1 ("Flight surgeon notified automatically through the relay"). However, SPEC §9.1 shows the same banner counting down "arrives in m:ss", so at the time the astronaut reads it the notice is still in transit. **Proposed wording everywhere: "An alert has been sent to the flight surgeon."** The rewrites below use that wording.
